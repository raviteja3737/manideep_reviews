## The review (verbatim)

Proof that your twenties are not the age to be mature or have everything figured out. It's the age to enjoy, make mistakes, and obsess over somebody you love.

I genuinely cannot describe this book in one word, so here's everything that comes to my mind when I think about it: love, friendship, parental pressure, conspiracy, betrayal, and, above all, the craziest revenge idea ever. Total *pyaar mein andha* lover-boy vibes, along with some proper Mumbaikar slang!

We follow Vihaan, whose life is nothing less than a roller coaster. After spending years blindly fulfilling his father's expectations, he finally gives up and decides to study Visual Arts at a university of his own choice.

That's where he falls in love with Raina. She starts dating another guy, breaks up with him, comes back to Vihaan, and then, thanks to some conspiracies, breaks things off with him too.

And just when you think things couldn't get worse for Vihaan, everybody starts giving up on him. Well, almost everybody. He still has Parth, his best friend, who would do literally anything for him in the blink of an eye.

Now, Vihaan wants to prove to his father and Raina that he's not a nobody and that he can actually do something with his life. So what does he do? Drops out of college and decides that he wants his face on every billboard in Ahmedabad. The cost? Just ₹10 crore!

And guess how much money he has in his pocket already? A grand total of ₹35. What a solid financial plan!

And that's where the craziest journey of bringing this absolutely insane revenge idea to life begins.

Honestly, this is one of the funniest books I've read in 2026. But it's not just about the humour. Somewhere between all the madness, it also talks about parental expectations, the pressure of trying to live up to them, the unexpected challenges life throws at you, how a genuinely loving heart can often be misunderstood, and, most importantly, having the courage to dream ridiculously big.

P.S. This isn't exactly the original text I typed, but this is what I was trying to express through my review. It's my first attempt at reviewing a book, so I'm still figuring things out. If you read it and have any suggestions or feedback, I'd be the happiest to hear them! Always open to learning and improving. ❤️

## A little note from me (verbatim)

This is genuinely my first attempt at reviewing a book. I've never really tried expressing my reading experience through a review before.

But Zidd was something else. It was probably the funniest and most chaotic book I've read in 2026. Reading it felt like exploring one unexpected situation after another, laughing at the madness, and enjoying every little twist.

I'm still figuring out how to put my thoughts into words, so this is also a learning experience for me.

If you've read my review, I'd genuinely love to know what you think. Be honest. I'm here to learn.
